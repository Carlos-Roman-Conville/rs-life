Would follow from `anatomy.tissues.connective-tissue-collagen` integrity falling (already driven by the pool below 300 mg), as a medicine wound-state reopening gated on integrity under the same first-loss threshold; evidence level 4 for the link (Lind, qualitative), level 5 for any daily chance or integrity cutoff because no source gives a rate or timing. (b) Gum disease: Lind and Francescone agree (swelling and bleeding, secondary periodontal infection, only in people with teeth); would follow from the same integrity state and the pool, level 4, onset only as the 4 to 12 week general window (Gandhi), no oral-specific timing. Tooth loss: Lind only ("teeth moft commonly become quite loofe, and often fall out", jaw caries rare); would follow from integrity falling further than for gum bleeding, level 4 for existence and 5 for how far, because no modern review mentions tooth loss. (c) Death: Pimentel and Francescone give "infection or sudden death"; Lind gives sudden death on moving or exertion and haemorrhage in the late stage; Francescone lists intracerebral haemorrhage and bleeding into the peritoneum, pericardium and adrenals as rare late features; Gandhi hypothesises a vasomotor cause with no frequency. Would be a `src/crisis.ts` crisis (cause such as `exposure:` or a new scurvy cause, `risk` null) opened at an integrity or pool floor and recorded as the cause-of-death ledger entry, level 4 for the causes and level 5 for the floor and any risk, since no case-fatality rate or time to death was found. (d) Stage: the only levels given are plasma under 20 micromol per L (non-specific), under 11.4 (specific), the existing 300 mg pool and the 4 to 12 week window; no source places reopening, tooth loss or death at a named level, so any ordering of these consequences along the integrity scale is level 5. Silent in every source opened, so a question for Rex: the rate or timing of wound reopening; the integrity or pool level at which teeth are lost; any scurvy case-fatality rate or time to death; whether the sudden-death trigger is exertion or a fixed hazard. Wikipedia's 1,300 deaths of about 2,000 on Anson's voyage is the only mortality figure and is second-hand, not all scurvy, and conflicts with Pearce's 188 of 1,854 surviving.
