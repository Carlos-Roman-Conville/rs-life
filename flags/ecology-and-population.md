## ecology and population (2530)

- UNSUPPORTED: ecology r, K, q and effort for any ground, ECOLOGY.md depletion_rules. The table gives the model and one recovery figure (reef fish 7 years, pelagics 3, turtles 56), not the Caribbean's r or K; the host supplies each stock. `recoveryYears` is the logistic's own ln 16 / r (2.77 / r), which the row rounds to 2.8 / r. There is no extinction threshold or Allee effect: a stock at zero stays at zero. The fished-out catch factor (x0.2) applies to `dailyCatch` (the per-fisher formula), not to a host's explicit catch amount.
- UNSUPPORTED: ecology price and duty consequences of a fished-out ground ("the port's fish price up", "the fisherman duty yields little"): economy and duties are the game's. The boucanier history row is a note, not a rule; hunting uses the same stock model with the host's rates.
- UNSUPPORTED: ecology soil rain and labour factors, ECOLOGY.md soil. The `yield` row names a rain and a labour factor but carries only the dry-year figure (under 60% of normal rain halves yields); `dryYearFactor` returns null for other rain and the host supplies both factors. A hurricane's destruction of standing cane and plantains, and the cattle-pen proximity of the manure row, are the host's. The index is clamped to [0, 1] (the table's own range); the amount cut by the clamp is not traced.
- NOTE: ecology soil table is marked confidence low, numbers placeholders, in the library itself; the numbers are used as written. "A decade" is 3,650 days.
- UNSUPPORTED: population rate rows. Birth rate, death rate (one rate; the elders' x2 is the row's), child sex split on reaching 15, catch rate of a disease and prosperity/reputation scales are the host's (Port Royal's stay in the game). The settler rate (x0.001 a day) is the row's.
- UNSUPPORTED: population adults to elders: the ageing row gives children to adults (1/5475 a day, 15 years) and "adults -> elders at 50" without a rate. The engine uses the cohort's width, 35 years (1/12,775 a day), which the table implies and does not state.
- UNSUPPORTED: population health recovery and epidemic effect on health. The food row lowers health 0.02 a day in famine; the table has no recovery rate and no health cost of an outbreak. Health stays where it is when food returns; the host sets it. Neither is the `cohorts.health` "epidemic state" modelled.
- UNSUPPORTED: population epidemic details. The exposure window is the disease row's maximum course day, an explicit pulse convention rather than a measured outbreak duration; `riskAt` selects only the risk of newly exposed batches. Incubation, recovery and fatal crisis timing use medicine's full integer-day distributions. Missing numeric incubation or course throws; missing numeric risk adds no deaths. Only a lifelong immunity row (medicine `immunityRule`) moves survivors into `seasonedShare`; partial immunity changes nothing, as in medicine. No treatment, quarantine or mortality-with-age (typhus) acts on cohorts; sick people do not lower births beyond the existing health input. Labour excludes incubation, recoveries and deaths.
- UNSUPPORTED: population migration out ("fear of fever x epidemic") and the unnamed recruit pool: leavers are host numbers; the transient pool is changed only by host arrivals and departures and is not subject to births, deaths or ageing. `enslaved` and `occupations` cohort fields are not modelled (the first is Rex's open question; the second has no row).
- NOTE (2626): Aggregation interface: medicine owns `diseaseCourse`, exported `rangePoint`, and `diseaseOutcomeDays`; population consumes their parsed ranges and exact daily outcome expectations. Exposure is once daily at host catch rate in [0, 1], from the not-yet-caught, initially unseasoned resident pool. Each batch fixes its risk at exposure. The deterministic 1,000-individual yellow-fever test compares attack rate, case fatality and mean symptomatic illness duration (ending at crisis counted as death or recovery) within three sample standard errors: sample SD / sqrt(n), with n = 1,000 for attack and the infected subset for conditional fatality/duration. This is verification, not validation.
- (2626) level 4: Approximation and declared gap: force of infection (catch rate) is a host input, not linked to prevalence. The maximum-course-day exposure pulse is not a transmission model; P3 transmission between bodies must replace it. Retain the mean-of-individuals verification test.
- (2626) unvalidated: Full outbreak attack rate and duration are not validated. Searched the supplied `validation.outbreak_records` rows in data/library/validation.json (2645's opened Angola 2016 and Philadelphia 1793 yellow-fever records and Shigella refugee-camp records). Angola's attack-rate row is null and it has no individual illness-duration measurement; Philadelphia's 100 days measures the death-count window, not an individual's illness. Shigella's 5-29 weeks also measures epidemic duration and exceeds this pulse. No source supplies a joint yellow-fever attack/fatality/illness-duration observation with uncertainty. Test with an independently observed cohort's daily exposure history, incident cases, crisis/death dates and recoveries, using the record's own uncertainty; do not substitute epidemic duration for illness duration or fit catch rate to claim predictive validation.
- (2626) report: Accuracy: exact closed-cohort first-infection expectations of medicine's chosen uniform incubation/course/crisis-day distributions, checked analytically and against 1,000 seeded individuals at three sample SE. The Angola suspected-case CFR (8.7%) lies inside the 5-10% yellow-fever range at riskAt 0.74; the confirmed-case CFR (13.7%) does not. These are different denominators, not interchangeable estimates. The source supplies no uncertainty band, so none is invented; range compatibility alone is not full outbreak validation. Limitations/open questions: uniform draws are assumptions, the force of infection and pulse duration remain approximations, no reinfection within an outbreak even for diseases without lifelong immunity, and the exposed cohort is fixed at outbreak start. Births/migration, competing background mortality and ageing are not tracked within exposure batches; cohorts with such changes are not an exact individual expectation, and deaths are capped at available residents. Seasoning remains a single adult share applied to the resident pool rather than disease-specific immunity.
