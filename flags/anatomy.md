- (3019) proposed pathway: CG-15, cell birth and death in tissue populations (library table `tissue_turnover`, 59 rows, 25 sources, keys TT-*; propose only, nothing built; src/tissue.ts was not present, so the TissuePopulation fields are taken from DESIGN §1.10: viable mass, turnover per day, senescent fraction). Evidence levels are my reading of PLATFORM §4, which was not in this task's read list, using the convention in these flags (3 = printed from or fitted to measurements in a stated population, 4 = approximation, model or compilation with a different definition, 5 = estimate, relayed or uncalibrated; "silent" = no level because no source). Law: dN/dt = B − D, written in viable mass M (kg) so that each flow is one transfer, `Δsource = −q`, `Δdestination = +q`, losses separate. (1) BIRTH B: `body.protein_store → tissue.viable_mass`, q = B·dt (kg of new-cell protein and water; the stem or progenitor pool in `realism.cellular.stem_cell_pools` sets the ceiling on B, it does not supply the mass, because a stem cell is a few cells and the new mass is food-derived). DEATH D: `tissue.viable_mass → waste.debris` (shed epidermis and gut cells, cleared red cells), q = D·dt, with k = D/M = the population's `turnover` field (per day) and k = 1/τ at steady state, so M holds when B = D. Any recycled amino acid or iron is a second transfer back; sources are silent on the recycled share. The energy to build cells is NOT a new term: Sender and Milo 2021 (TT-SENDER-2021) put it at 4.5 to 5.3 kJ per g protein, 16 to 19 kcal/day, about 1% of whole-body resting rate (3% of the gut's), already inside a measured resting rate, so adding it would count it twice (level 3 for the figures, 4 for the statement, as 3018 did for synthesis heat). (2) RATES k, each as `turnover` and each with τ from the census unless stated (level 3 unless stated): red cells τ = 116 d (Sender; Higgins and Mahadevan TT-HIGGINS-2010 about 120 d; children 60 to 80 d); neutrophils 0.9 d in blood plus 5.7 d marrow transit; small-intestine epithelium 5.3 d, colon 3.4 d, stomach 13 d; epidermal keratinocytes 25 to 38 d (basal layer to loss of viable cells); cardiomyocytes 1% per year at 25 falling to 0.45% at 75, fewer than half exchanged in a life (Bergmann 2009 TT-BERGMANN-2009; 2015 gives under 1% in adults, cardiomyocyte number fixed perinatally; non-myocytes endothelial over 15%/yr, mesenchymal under 4%/yr), so k_cm(age) is a table, not a constant; hepatocytes: mean age under 3 years and diploid birth rate over 7 times the polyploid (Heinke 2022 TT-HEINKE-2022, level 3), about one turnover per year for diploid cells and ageing not changing renewal (press-release relay, level 5); bone: remodelling cycle about 200 d cancellous, 120 d cortical, resorption 30 to 40 d, formation about 150 d (Eriksen TT-ERIKSEN-2010), cortical turnover 2 to 3%/yr (Clarke TT-CLARKE-2008), modelled as a coupled pair `bone.matrix → mineral_pool` (resorption, D) then `mineral_pool → bone.matrix` (formation, B) so mineral is conserved, with lacuna depth 60 µm young and 40 µm older as the size of the unrefilled step (level 3 for the depths, silent for the refill efficiency); neurons: neocortex B = 0 (not detectable in adults, Bhardwaj TT-BHARDWAJ-2006, level 3), hippocampus 700 new neurons/day and 1.75%/yr of a renewing third (Spalding TT-SPALDING-2013) contradicted by Sorrells 2018 (TT-SORRELLS-2018, extremely rare in adults): both are named, not reconciled, and the proposal is B = 0 unless a host opts in (level 4). Whole-body check on the sum of all D: 330 billion cells and 80 ± 20 g per day for a 70 kg adult (level 3; about 0.1% of body mass per day), which any filled-in tissue table should reproduce. (3) REGENERATION after loss: B is raised, not a new process. Partial hepatectomy: two-thirds removed, 60% of hepatocytes make one round of DNA synthesis (peak 24 h rat, about 36 h mouse), a smaller second round restores number, mass back in 5 to 7 d (rodent) and 8 to 15 d (human), a small apoptosis wave trims overshoot (Michalopoulos 2007 TT-MICHALOPOULOS-2007, level 3 for rodent timings, 4 for the human range as the review's own summary). So `body.protein_store → liver.viable_mass` with B = r·(M_target − M) fitted to the 8 to 15 d endpoint is my form, level 5, and needs a target (original mass) held in the population; the sources give timings and fractions, not r. Living-donor volumes (81.4% of pre-operative at day 30, relayed, row marked `open`) are an independent check once the page is opened. The source of the new cells is mature hepatocytes, not a stem pool (oval cells only when hepatocytes cannot divide), so liver needs no entry in `stem_cell_pools` (level 3). Skeletal muscle: B for a fibre is repair by satellite cells, `satellite pool → fibre.viable_mass`, silent on the rate in humans; human satellite cells per type II fibre 0.080 young (20 y) against 0.044 elderly (76 y), n = 8 each (Verdijk TT-VERDIJK-2007, level 3), mouse hindlimb fibres taking satellite nuclei about 20% over 6 months, steady by 12 weeks (Pawlikowski TT-PAWLIKOWSKI-2015, level 4, mouse). (4) STEM POOLS and age (`realism.cellular.stem_cell_pools`: pool size S, cycle time, output limit on B): haematopoietic, one division per 40 weeks (25 to 50), pool size rises from birth to adolescence then plateaus (Catlin TT-CATLIN-2011, level 3), and in people over 65 the pool is more frequent, less quiescent, myeloid-biased with lymphoid progenitors falling (Pang TT-PANG-2011, relayed, level 5), so total output does NOT simply fall with age, the mix changes; intestinal Lgr5 cells at the crypt base generate all lineages in 60 d (Barker TT-BARKER-2007, mouse, level 4); epidermal progenitors divide about weekly and slow-cycling stem cells about every 3 months in mouse tail (Alcolea and Jones TT-ALCOLEA-2014, level 4, mouse only). Output demonstrably falls with age only for cardiomyocyte renewal and satellite-cell content.
