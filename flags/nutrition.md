Evidence levels follow the convention in these flags because PLATFORM.md section 4 was outside this task's read list, so the keeper should confirm: 3 a measured study read at abstract or table level, 4 a review median, secondary relay, regulatory standard or survey estimate, 5 no source. Every transfer is one physical flow with `Δsource = −q`, `Δdestination = +q` (PLATFORM §7a). Today `eatMeal` sends the unabsorbed part of each pooled nutrient and of food water to a sink named `unabsorbed`, and unabsorbed energy to `waste`, but `absorbedFraction` returns 1 for energy and for water whenever gut function is 1, so a healthy body currently leaves no energy or water residue; nothing records faeces or urine as a store. (1) FAECAL DRY MASS: food to `faeces`, q = unabsorbed solids, kg dry; plus body to `faeces` for the endogenous part (bacteria, sloughed cells, secretions), routed through `bodyMassDelta` (the sole body-mass writer, 2623). Median 29 g per day (12 to 81; 28 high-income, 38 low-income) of 128 g wet (Rose 2015 review, level 4); UK adults 106 g wet (Cummings 1992, 220 adults, level 3). Bacteria are 25 to 54% of dry solids in the review's abstract as the search result showed it, so dry mass is not only unabsorbed food; no page read says how much is endogenous body mass. (2) FAECAL WATER: food water unabsorbed (the existing `unabsorbed` water transfer) plus body water to `faeces` through `physiology.addBodyWater` as mass:water, q = 0.75 × wet mass − unabsorbed food water, floored at 0 (water 75%, range 63 to 86, Rose, level 4; about 96 g per day at 128 g wet). At near-equal fibre the vegetarian stool held 78.9% water against 72.6% (Reddy via Rose, level 4), so the water share is not a fixed constant. No page gave gut secretion volumes. (3) FIBRE AND DIET: wet mass = 101.3 + 2.96 × fibre g per day (±1.13 on the slope; Rose Figure 2, level 4), correlation 0.84 over 26 groups (Cummings, level 3), vegan 225 g at 47 g fibre against omnivore 153 g at 23 g; dry mass barely moves (omnivore 27.1 g, vegetarian 27.9 g, Van Faassen via Rose), so the extra wet mass is retained water, not extra solid. Needs a fibre content on each food, which this task did not check. (4) FAECAL NITROGEN: food to `faeces`, q = unabsorbed N of each pooled protein, g N. 1.8 g per day (0.9 to 4.9; Rose, level 4); 1.38 g, 19% of a 7.16 g N intake (19 Korean women, 김주연 1987, level 3); N absorption about 75% at 44 g protein against about 85% at 85 g (8 women, 구재옥 1988, level 3), so the unabsorbed share falls as protein rises. Rose's faecal protein median 6.3 g over 1.8 g N implies a factor of 3.5, not the textbook 6.25, because the two medians come from different study sets (n = 7 and 18); no conversion factor from protein to N was sourced here. (5) URINE WATER: body water to `urine`, q = urine volume, kg: median 1.42 L (0.8 to 2.45; Rose Table 10, level 4); fluid intake explained 78% of its variation in one sample (Parker and Gallagher via Rose). Physiology's existing daily water ledger may already carry a urine term; it was not read, so check it before adding this one. (6) URINE NITROGEN: body protein pool to `urine`, q = f × absorbed N, g N, f = 0.854 ± 0.036 of N intake (김주연 1987, level 3) and 'about 80%' (Kipnis via Rose, level 4: the primary abstract gives no recovery figure); absolute 11 g N median (2 to 35; Rose Table 8, level 4) or 6.03 g (김주연 1987); the 35 g upper end is a range of study means across diets and the page summary did not show which study gave it, so treat the range as uncertain. Urea is 75.2% of urinary N (level 3), rising from 79% to 85% as protein doubled (level 3). Bingham and Cummings 1985: urine N varies 11 to 18% within a person against 14 to 26% for intake, so one day's urine N is a noisy reading of that day's intake. (7) FAECAL ENERGY: the existing food to `waste` transfer keeps its form but its fraction needs a source: 4.6% of gross energy lost on a control diet and 8.0% with 16 g per day refined cellulose (100 minus the 95.4% and 92.0% digestibilities, Slavin and Marlett 1980, 7 women, level 3; with fibre energy subtracted digestibility did not differ, 96.1 vs 95.6%). Median 132 kcal per day (49 to 347; Rose, level 4); the review's 4115 kcal per kg dry solids (29 g × 4.115 = 119 kcal) lets faecal energy be derived from the dry-mass transfer in (1) instead of carried separately. Miles 1988 (12 men, 37 g vs 16 g fibre) and Baer 1997 (17 subjects) report lower energy availability on higher fibre but their abstracts carry no faecal energy number. (8) HORSE (565 kg Finnhorse mares, 4-day total collection, Saastamoinen 2021, level 3, one breed): fresh faeces 14.4 to 19.3 kg per day (18.7 on hay), faecal dry matter 3.2 to 3.6 kg, about 19 to 22% dry (80% water); urine 10.9 to 14.3 L; faecal N 43 to 52 g; urinary N 85 (hay) to 123 g unacidified, 64 to 104 g acidified. Faecal dry matter was 39 to 48% of dry matter intake (0.44 on hay, computed), a usable transfer rule: feed to `faeces` at (1 − digestibility) × dry matter intake. Scottish standard: 24 L excreta and 58 g N per day with no body weight (level 4); a Dutch owner survey: 228 ± 134 g N per day at 542 kg (a calculation from reported feeding, level 4). Total N differs fourfold between sources (58, about 136, 228 g). WHAT THE SOURCES LEAVE SILENT: the endogenous share of faecal solids and N; gut secretion and reabsorption volumes; urinary energy (urea, creatinine) in kcal; skin and sweat N (김주연 1987 reports none); a protein-to-N factor; the faecal energy share on a mixed diet whose energy intake is stated; and any horse faeces-and-urine review figure with a stated body weight. In Saastamoinen's own tables faecal plus urinary N (136 g on hay unacidified, 115 acidified) exceeds the roughly 110 g N intake, so the authors computed no balance; do not close a horse N ledger against that paper. Every transfer is one physical flow with `Δsource = −q`, `Δdestination = +q` (PLATFORM §7a). Proposal: a new colon stage after the small-gut `absorbedFraction`, with a gut type per species (simple, hindgut, foregut). (1) FERMENTED CARBOHYDRATE: food to `colon`, q = g of fibre, resistant starch and unabsorbed sugars that reach the caecum times a fermented fraction f, in g. Human arrival 20 to 40 g/day, up to about 50 (Elia and Cummings 2007, abstract, level 4); f from 0 to 100% by substrate (FAO Paper 66, level 4); measured apparent fibre digestibility 46.6% (Wisker) and 67 to 82% (Baer), both relayed by Hervik and Svihus 2019 (level 4); in vitro acid yield 10 to 40 g per 100 g fibre (level 4). The unfermented remainder goes `colon` to `faeces` (the CG-6 stool, 3016 line). No source gives f for resistant starch or by fibre type in vivo, so per-substrate f stays open. (2) ENERGY: two routes that must agree. Top-down: 8 kJ per g fermented fibre for labelling (FAO Paper 66 Recommendation 8, level 4), against a measured spread of −20 to +10 kJ/g across human studies and −2.1 (coarse rye), 4.9 (whole rye), 5 (sugar beet), 7.5 (citrus), 11.9 (inulin) (Hervik and Svihus 2019, relayed, level 4); the label value is a convention, not a constant, and f must not be applied on top of it twice. Bottom-up: acid moles per g times combustion energy; inulin gives 9.13 acetate, 0.72 propionate, 1.31 butyrate mmol/g (Boets 2015, model-derived, level 3) times −875.16, −1527.3, −2183.5 kJ/mol (NIST, liquid acids, level 4), 11.95 kJ/g by my arithmetic (level 5), which agrees with the 11.9 net figure for inulin; 300 mmol/day at 2.0:0.5:0.5 gives about 361 kJ/day, about 4% of an assumed 2,000 kcal, below McNeil's 5 to 10% (1984 review, level 4) and Bergman's about 10% (1990 review, level 4). Transfers per fermented g: `colon` chemical energy to `body.intake_kcal` (the acid energy absorbed), to `faeces` (acids lost, 5 to 10%, Boets intro, relayed, level 4; and bacterial mass, no number found), to `room air` as gas chemical energy (H2, CH4, below) and to heat; the terms must sum to the energy of the fermented carbohydrate, the same closure as the CG-13 test. One `intake_kcal` bucket ignores where the energy is used: Bergman says most butyrate is oxidised in the gut wall, the liver takes propionate (much to glucose) and peripheral tissue takes acetate (level 4); an organ split would need circulation and metabolism states and is not proposed. (3) ACID ABSORPTION: acids in the colon to blood at the rates in McNeil 1978 (rectal, 5.2, 1.8, 1.9 umol per cm2 per h, level 3, rectum not colon) and Cummings 1987 (portal 375, hepatic 148, peripheral 79 umol/l, level 3); Boets assumed 40%, 10% and 5% of acetate, propionate and butyrate reach the circulation, an assumption not a measurement (level 5). Acid ratio 75:15:10 to 40:40:20 (Bergman, level 4), human inulin 82:6:12 (Boets, level 3). (4) GAS, through the room air port: `bodyGasFlows` (src/respiration.ts) takes only metabolic watts, so H2, CH4 and fermentation CO2 would be new terms on the same `GasFlows`, from `colon` to `room air`, in mol (mass by the chemistry library's molar masses, not read). Measured: H2 excreted per g lactulose 17.3 mL (non-methanogens) and 7.9 (methanogens), H2 plus CH4 34.9 mL/g (Christl 1992, level 3); pectin gives only 14.1% and banana starch 38.6% of the lactulose H2, so hydrogen is not a measure of fermentation (level 3); 58% of excreted H2 and CH4 leaves in the breath, 25% to 65% by rate, the rest as flatus (level 3), so the breath share goes through the lung path and the rest through a flatus path; baseline 35 mL/24 h (level 3). Most hydrogen made is consumed: absolute H2 80 mL per g glucose against net 1/64 to 1/900 of that (Strocchi and Levitt 1992, in vitro, level 3), with sulfate reducers and methanogens as sinks (Christl, level 3). Mass balance: the hexose balances (to acetate 2 CO2 and 4 [2H]; to propionate −2 [2H], no CO2; to butyrate 2 CO2 and 2 [2H]; CO2 + 4 [2H] gives CH4 + 2 H2O; Mahmood 2020 Table 1, a rumen paper, level 4) give for inulin 11.75 mmol CO2 and 18.85 mmol [2H] per g before sinks (my arithmetic, level 5), so CH4 cannot exceed 4.7 mmol/g; the measured 34.9 mL/g H2 plus CH4 is about 1.4 mmol, so the sinks, not the stoichiometry, set the hydrogen and methane amounts. Four of ten subjects in Christl excreted methane, a trait of the person; no population share found. (5) SPECIES: foregut ruminant acid energy share about 70% (Bergman, level 4); other species 20 to 30% (Bergman, species not named in the abstract, level 4). Horse: acid concentrations by segment, caecum 84.4, ventral colon 114.8, rectum 44.4 mmol/l, acetate:propionate:butyrate about 3.7:1:0.5, 23 horses (Santo 2026, level 3); share of maintenance energy 60 to 70% (Santo discussion, refs not opened, level 5) or 50 to 70% (Rutgers, uncited, level 5), against Bergman's 20 to 30% for other species, so the horse share is unsettled and should not be coded as one number; whole-tract crude fibre digestibility 32.2 to 64.8% on five diets, 4 horses (Zicarelli 2023, level 3, not hindgut-only); methane energy 2 kJ per g crude fibre (Ebert 2020, an applied estimate, level 4); gas 1.69 mmol per mmol acid in vitro (Zicarelli, level 4).
